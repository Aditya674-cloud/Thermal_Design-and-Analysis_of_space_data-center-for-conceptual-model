# 5.1 Thermal Energy Balance
Thermal balance is the equilibrium state where a systems energy inputs equal its outputs, resulting in temperature stability.( https://sustainability-directory.com/term/thermal-balance/)
It represents a dynamic harmony between energy flows, ensuring that the rate of heat production matches the rate of heat loss.
For steady-state operation: 
$$Q_gen=Q_rad$$
Where:
$$Q_gen$$= heat generated by the computing module (W)
$$Q_rad$$= heat rejected by the radiator(W)
Here we need according to above equation both equal of 150W (Given) so it should be: - 
$$Q_gen=Q_rad=150W$$
So, it means
If the radiator is rejecting less than 150W, heat accumulates and its temperature rises.
If it is rejecting more than 150W, the radiator can cool until the radiation loss becomes equal to the generated heat.

# 5.2 Radiative Heat Rejection
The system in which model will carryout operation is considered to operate in space, there is no surrounding atmosphere available for conventional external convective heat rejection.
Therefore, the radiator rejects thermal energy primarily through thermal radiation.
Using the Stefan-Boltzmann relation:
$$Qrad=ϵσA(Trad^4-Tspace^4 )$$
Where,
ϵ = 0.92 ,σ= 5.67*10^(-8)  W/(m^2 k^4 ),A = 0.48m^2,Tspace = 3k


Where,
  Qrad= radiative heat rejection(W)
   ϵ= radiator surface emissivity
   σ= Stefan-Boltzmann constant 
   A = effective radiating area(m^2)
   Trad= radiator absolute temperature (K)
   Tspace= effective radiative sink temperature (K)
For the baseline analytical model, deep space is approximated as an effective radiative sink at 3K

# 5.3 Equilibrium Radiator Temperature 
Therefore:
$$Qrad=ϵσA(Trad^4-Tspace^4 )$$
Rearranging:

$$150=0.92*5.67*10^- 8*0.48*(T_r^4 ad-3^4 )$$


$$T_r^4 ad-81=150/(0.92*5.67*10^- 8*0.48)=5.99*10^9$$

Therefore,

$$T_r ad=(5.99*10^9 )^(1/4)$$
Trad = 278.19K
Which is nearly 5°C.
This is the key result of this analytical model which convey under the baseline assumptions, the calculated equilibrium radiator temperature is approximately 278.2K. At this temperature, the radiative heat rejection balances the 150W heat generated by the computing module.


# 5.4 Initial Radiator temperature Assessment
Initially the temperature radiated was considered as 350K which results Qradiated greater than Qgenerated. Which is shown below;

$$Qrad=ϵσA(Trad^4-Tspace^4 )$$
Trad = 350k

$$Qrad=0.92*5.67*10^- 8*0.48*(350^4-3^4 )$$

Qrad = 375W
This means the radiator has sufficient rejection capacity at 350K, but 350K is not the equilibrium temperature for the baseline 150W heat load.
Consequently, rather than assuming the radiator operating temperature, the equilibrium temperature was determined from the steady-state energy balance.

# 5.6 Radiator Mass Estimation
The radiator mass is calculated using:
m=ρV
For a thin radiator plate:
m=ρAt
Where:
m = radiator mass(kg)
ρ= radiator material density $$(kg/m^3)$$
A = radiator area $$(m^2)$$
t = radiator thickness (m)
For aluminum:
ρ= 2700 $$kg/m^3$$
t = 0.002m
Area (Given) =$$0.48m^2$$
Therefore,
m = (2700)(0.48)(0.002)
m = 2.592kg
This is the radiator mass for the area assumption.
