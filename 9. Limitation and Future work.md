# 9.1 Limitations
Till here the paper shows a conceptual thermal design study. The analytical model which drawn
in SOLIDWORKS and analyzed in ANSYS consists of constant deep space sink or temperature 3K. Here
its noticeable that there is no consideration of space level environmental effects such as 
radiation, planets infrared radiation etc. 

In fact the present design model represents a steady state heat load of 150W and does not
investigate transient variation in server power consumption. The thermal behavior during analysis
of starting, peak computational loading and changing operating conditions therefore remains outside
the scope of the current study.  

The numerical data in this analysis is also assumed which is dependent on material properties, 
thermal contact, geometry, and boundary conditions used in ANSYS. The model is mainly intended
to evaluate the thermal feasibility and heat transfer behavior of the proposed conceptual design
rather than provided flight qualified thermal data.

Furthermore, the current study does not include experimental validation or hardware testing.
Experimental testing under representative thermal vacuum conditions would be required to validate
the analytical and numerical predictions.

# 9.2 Future work
Future work will be on increasing the accuracy and designing actual thermal model and evaluating
the system under realistic spacecraft operating conditions. The future analysis will also get
introduction to actual solar radiation, planetary infrared radiation, and spacecraft surface in 
environment while studying.

Further also going to optimize in radiator dimensions, number of radiator panels, surface
emissivity and thermal path which can be performed to minimize radiator mass while maintaining
the thermal rejection capability under required conditions. A more detailed model of cooling
interface and thermal contacts can also be developed to reduce uncertainties related to thermal
resistance.

 A scaled prototype could be tested under controlled thermal vacuum conditions to compare measured 
 temperatures and heat rejection with the analytical and numerical predictions. Such validation
 would improve confidence in the proposed thermal architecture and provide a basis for further
 development toward a spacecraft compatible thermal management system.
