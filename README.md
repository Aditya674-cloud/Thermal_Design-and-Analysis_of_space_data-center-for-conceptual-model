# Thermal_management_of_space_data-center

## We had project seminar in our institute and the topic chosen by me was studying how heat gets radiated from data center in space and for that i considered study of STARCLOUD-4 as a reference concept and focuses on understanding the basic principles of thermal management, particularly heat transfer through conduction and heat rejection through thermal radiation.

### Objectives 

1. To able to understand basic principle of space datacenter
2. To design reference model in design software
3. To study the STAR CLOUD-4 concept as a reference for space-based data center thermal management.
4. To perform basic thermal calculations for heat generation and radiative heat rejection

### Methodology

The project was carried out through the following stages:

Study - Reference Concept - CAD Model - Thermal Calculations - Simulation - Results - Conclusion

1. Studied the concept of data centers operating in space.
2. Used STAR CLOUD-4 as a reference study.
3. Created a simplified representation of the data center and radiator system.
4. Assumed a representative server heat generation of 150 W.
5. Calculated the required radiating area and radiative heat rejection using the Stefan–Boltzmann equation.
6. Studied the effect of radiator emissivity and temperature on heat rejection.
7. Performed a basic thermal simulation using ANSYS.

### Outcomes

At the end of the project, the following are the things i learned:

1. Developed a basic understanding of thermal management in space.
2. Studied the thermal-management approach of the STAR CLOUD-4 reference concept.
3. Developed a simplified CAD model of a space data-center thermal system.
4. Performed basic calculations for heat generation and radiative heat rejection.
5. Studied how radiator area, emissivity, and temperature influence heat rejection.
6. Gained practical exposure to ANSYS thermal analysis and interpretation of temperature and heat-flux results.
7. Developed an understanding of the challenges involved in managing heat from future space-based computing systems.
