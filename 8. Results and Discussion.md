# 8.10 Overall thermal performance and design discussion

The overall thermal performance of the proposed space data center cooling concept was evaluated by combining the analytical thermal calculations with the numerical results obtained using ANSYS. The proposed system consists of a server encloser connected to cooling tubes and four external radiator panels, providing a total radiator area of 0.48m^2. The analytical model considered a heat generation rate of 150W, radiator emissivity of 0.92, and a deep space sink temperature of 3K.
The analytical investigation established the fundamental thermal behavior of the proposed radiator system. For the selected radiator area emissivity, the equilibrium 278.19K. This result indicates that the proposed radiator area is theoretically capable of rejecting the specified heat load through thermal radiation under the assumed idealized deep space conditions.
As the radiator temperature increases, the radiative heat rejection increases according to the fourth power dependence on absolute temperature. For example, at approximately 375W, which is substantially higher model predicts a radiative heat rejection of approximately 375W, which is substantially higher than the nominal 150W heat load considered in the baseline design. This demonstrates that radiator operating temperature is an important design parameter in determining the available thermal rejection capacity.
The emissivity analysis indicates that increasing the radiator surface emissivity improves radiative heat rejection and reduces the equilibrium temperature required for a given heat load. The selected emissivity of 0.92 therefore provides a high radiation performance surface condition for the conceptual radiator design. The results demonstrate that surface properties should be considered with radiator geometry when design a passive thermal management system for space. 
The steady state of ANSYS used to analysis temperature and heat flux within the proposed thermal model. The temperature solution shows the thermal gradient between the heat generation server box, the cooling components, and the radiator panels. And the heat flux distribution showed higher heat flux regions around the thermal conduction path. 
The mesh convergence analysis showed that progressively refining the radiator mesh from 10mm to 4mm convey that maximum temperature remains same that is 382.77°C. But seen that there are variations in readings in minimum temperature, nodes, and mesh elements.
Overall, the analytical results shows the theoretical feasibility of the given radiator concept for rejecting the specified 150W thermal load under the assumed deep space radiation condition. Here the ANSYS results provides temperature and heat flux distribution and the analytical model by showing internal thermal behavior of the given configuration.
Here, in this present study its considered only a conceptual thermal analysis rather than a complete spacecraft to space data center thermal analysis by looking forward sun and earth behavior. Similarly the numerical model depends on the defined material properties, thermal contacts, boundary conditions and radiation assumptions. Therefore, further validation and refinement of the model are required before the design can be considered for an actual space-flight application.
